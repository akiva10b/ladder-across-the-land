וְהִנֵּה סֻלָּם מֻצָּב אַרְצָה וְרֹאשׁוֹ מַגִּיעַ הַשָּׁמָיְמָה

A Ladder Across the Land

Jacob, Beit El, Jerusalem and Shiloh. A geographic reading of Parashat Vayetze that follows Rashi and the midrash, and asks where the house of God really stood.

3 parts · 13 scenes Be'er Sheva · Ebla · Beit El · Jerusalem · Shiloh · Haran Scroll to follow the map
Prologue
01

Jeroboam's question

At the opening of the parasha Jacob discovers the place of the Shekhinah at Beit El. He names it the house of God and the gate of heaven, and vows to build there on the stone he set up as a pillar. Later, in Laban's house, God appears to him as "the God of Beit El".

מַה נּוֹרָא הַמָּקוֹם הַזֶּה, אֵין זֶה כִּי אִם בֵּית אֱ-לֹהִים וְזֶה שַׁעַר הַשָּׁמָיִםGenesis 28:17

Read plainly, this hands Jeroboam son of Nevat his argument when the kingdom splits. The proper sanctuary is not David and Solomon's Jerusalem but Jacob's Beit El. Return to the source. The foreign altars Solomon's wives built in Jerusalem only strengthen the case.

וַיָּשֶׂם אֶת הָאֶחָד בְּבֵית אֵלI Kings 12:29

Chazal and Rashi seem to have felt the same pressure. Their readings carry Jerusalem to Beit El.

02

The whole land, folded under him

Rashi reads Jacob's encounter in two verses. Jacob lay at Beit El, yet the entire land lay beneath him, Jerusalem and Mount Moriah included. And "the place" he came upon is the place named elsewhere: Mount Moriah, which Abraham saw from afar.

קיפל הקב"ה כל ארץ ישראל תחתיו, רמז לו שתהא נוחה ליכבש לבניוRashi on Genesis 28:13
ויפגע במקום — לא הזכיר הכתוב באיזה מקום, אלא במקום הנזכר במקום אחר, הוא הר המוריהRashi on Genesis 28:11
Part I · The journey
03

Flight north, past Moriah

Working through Jacob's chronology, Rashi concludes that after taking the blessing Jacob hid for fourteen years in the study house of Shem and Ever. Following Rav Yoel Bin-Nun, the author locates that study house at Ebla (Tell Mardikh) in northern Syria.

Fleeing Esau in haste, Jacob had no time to stop and pray on Mount Moriah. He passed it by.

04

Fourteen years without lying down

וישכב במקום ההוא — לשון מיעוט, באותו מקום שכב, אבל ארבע עשרה שנים ששימש בבית עבר לא שכב בלילה, שהיה עוסק בתורהRashi on Genesis 28:11

The author reads this as a vow more than as unusual diligence. David swore he would not lie down until he found a resting place for the Shekhinah, and he dozed standing "like a horse" until midnight. Jacob, too, did not lie down until the night he found the place.

אִם אֶתֵּן שְׁנַת לְעֵינָי, לְעַפְעַפַּי תְּנוּמָה, עַד אֶמְצָא מָקוֹם לַה׳, מִשְׁכָּנוֹת לַאֲבִיר יַעֲקֹבPsalms 132:4–5 · Berakhot 3b
05

Back south, and the mountain comes to meet him

Before going on to Laban, Jacob turns back south to pray where his fathers prayed. He knows the area around the mountain but not its exact spot. When he reaches Beit El, the mountain comes to him.

אומר אני שנעקר הר המוריה ובא לכאן... שבא בית המקדש לקראתו עד בית אלRashi on Genesis 28:17 · Chullin 91b

The puzzle in the midrash is the scale. Why would God shorten a journey of some 800 km by only 15? Because the journey being shortened was the return from Ebla. The detour south was Jacob's own search for the place.

06

On to Haran

From Beit El Jacob continues north and east to Laban's house. There God appears to him again and names Himself by the place.

אָנֹכִי הָאֵ-ל בֵּית אֵל אֲשֶׁר מָשַׁחְתָּ שָּׁם מַצֵּבָהGenesis 31:13
Part II · The ladder
07

A ladder with two legs

הסולם הזה עומד בבאר שבע ואמצע שיפועו מגיע כנגד בית המקדשRashi on Genesis 28:17

The usual picture is a single slanted ladder, like a fire ladder, with its foot in Be'er Sheva and its head in the sky over Beit El. That leaves the midpoint meaningless, and Jerusalem is not halfway between them anyway. It is close to Beit El and far from Be'er Sheva.

Picture instead a household stepladder with two legs. Its "middle of the slope" is the apex, where the climb turns into the descent: the gate of heaven. The legs need not be equal, so the apex can stand over Jerusalem.

The angels of the Land of Israel climb from Be'er Sheva as Jacob leaves it, which is why the city's splendor departed with him. They enter heaven above Moriah. The angels of outside the Land come down the other leg to Beit El, Jacob's last point in the Land.

08

Jerusalem at Jacob's heart

Lay Jacob beneath the ladder, with the land folded under him. His feet are planted at Be'er Sheva. His head, and his thoughts, are at Beit El. The apex, the gate of heaven, stands over Jerusalem.

The author places Jacob's Beit El about 10 km north of today's. Measure it, and the gate of heaven falls over Jacob's heart.

With today's Beit El (Beitin) instead, Jerusalem would fall about 19% of the way down, at the throat. Straight-line distances between approximate sites.

Part III · Shiloh
10

Two Beit Els

On the plain meaning of the verses, Jacob's house of God is at Beit El, not Jerusalem. The author suggests "Beit El" is a name that more than one place could carry.

Abraham camped between Beit El and Ai. That Beit El seems always to have borne the name, and is identified with the Arab village of Beitin, east of today's settlement.

Jacob gave the name to a place previously called Luz. That may be a different site, further north, in the hills overlooking Shiloh from the south. There is an identification there that may fit.

11

When Beit El meant Shiloh

וַיָּבֹאוּ בֵית אֵל וַיִּבְכּוּ... וַיִּשְׁאֲלוּ בְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל בַּה׳, וְשָׁם אֲרוֹן בְּרִית הָאֱ-לֹהִים בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם, וּפִינְחָס בֶּן אֶלְעָזָר בֶּן אַהֲרֹן עֹמֵד לְפָנָיוJudges 20:26–28

The commentators rightly explain that Israel gathered at Shiloh, since the Ark and Pinchas were there. This also explains why Joshua set the Mishkan at Shiloh, a town with no prior distinction in the text.

It may be the meaning of Jacob's obscure blessing to Judah. Rule stays with Judah until God rests His presence at Shiloh, the place Jacob knew from his dream. Once the Ark arrives, God leads Israel, not one tribe.

לֹא יָסוּר שֵׁבֶט מִיהוּדָה וּמְחֹקֵק מִבֵּין רַגְלָיו עַד כִּי יָבֹא שִׁילֹהGenesis 49:10
12

The right hand on Ephraim

Jacob gave Shiloh to Joseph, his chosen son and Rachel's firstborn, whom he raised for priestly service as "the nazir among his brothers", the way Hannah would raise Samuel.

וַיִּשְׁלַח יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת יְמִינוֹ וַיָּשֶׁת עַל רֹאשׁ אֶפְרַיִם וְהוּא הַצָּעִירGenesis 48:14

The right hand points south. Ephraim would receive the southern part of Joseph's inheritance, the part that holds Shiloh. Rashi's own geography fits: Jerusalem on the border of Judah and Benjamin, Beit El on the border of Benjamin and Joseph.

13

Where Rachel and Leah meet

Why did Jacob not name Jerusalem, greater than Shiloh, given to Judah and Benjamin? Perhaps this is what Chazal meant when they said Jacob sought to reveal the end and it was hidden from him. His prophecy reached as far as Shiloh and no further.

Perhaps that was the price of his unchecked preference for Joseph and Rachel. God chose both Rachel and Leah, who together built the house of Israel. And He chose Jerusalem, where Rachel and Leah are joined in Benjamin and Judah.
